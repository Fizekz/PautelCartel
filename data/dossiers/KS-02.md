Kelly's past strength here suggests split-ticket voters exist, but she is not on the ballot. Raters converged on Likely R, which has historically meant roughly 85–90% for the favourite even in wave years. That puts Coover at about 15%, close to the market's 19.5%. I see no specific evidence that the market is wrong in Coover's favour.

**Missed evidence (cuts both ways)**
- The DCCC poll leans D, and its own memo shows it. It says respondents recall supporting Trump by +16, but KS-02 actually went Trump +20 (memo text; lean_2026.csv gives 60.0% R two-party). That is a sample about 4 points too Democratic, which puts the poll nearer Schmidt +6 to +7.
- The same memo has Hamilton +2 and Holscher +1 inside KS-02.
- But the statewide Senate race really is close, which suggests KS-02 is not a blowout either. KS-SEN polls, all from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Kansas?oldid=1383150596:
  - Emerson, Oct 26-28, 800 LV: Hamilton 50, Marshall 47.
  - SSRS, Oct 20-26, 838 LV: Hamilton 49, Marshall 45.
  - WaPo/SSPG, Oct 22-26: Hamilton 52, Marshall 43.
  - RedBox (D), Oct 20-23: Marshall 46, Hamilton 45.
  - Kalshi KS-SEN: Hamilton 0.505.
- Kansas went Trump 57.16-41.04 (+16.1) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Kansas), so KS-02 is about 3.5-4 points redder than the state.
- A statewide D+3 average implies roughly R+1 in KS-02 at the Senate level. Schmidt (incumbent, former AG) should run several points ahead of Marshall, which gives about Schmidt +5 to +7. That points to about 17-20% for Coover, essentially the market price.
- No outside-spending figures were found, from me or from the original dossier.

**Bottom line:** I found no mispricing. The dossier's 0.16 slightly underweights how close the statewide race is.
