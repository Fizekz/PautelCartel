## Reasoning
This is the most poll-backed of the R-held "Likely R" seats in this set. Two independent polls fall within ±4 of a tie, one with Jones ahead, and Trump is underwater in the district. Two major raters moved off Solid R in October, Jones is a credible, well-endorsed candidate, and the national environment is about D+9. Against that, Hill has a 5:1 cash-on-hand edge, the most recent poll has him up 4, and the 2020 polls badly understated him. All raters still say Likely R, which historically means about an 85-90% hold. I put Jones at about 25%, versus the market's 17.5%. That is a modest, low-confidence underpricing.

**Missed evidence (cuts against Jones):**

  - 2010: Griffin +12 in the poll, about +20 in the result.
  - 2014: Hays (D) **+4.5** (Oct 15) in the poll; Hill won by about 8. That is a miss of about 12 points toward the Democrat.
  - 2020: Hill +0.5 in the poll, +10.7 in the result.
  - 2012 (about R+4) and 2018 (about R+4 to R+6) missed the other way.
  - Net bias is about 4 to 5 points toward the D. The Aug Jones +4 corrected for it is about a tie, and the newer Emerson has Hill +4.
- The 2018 analogue: Hill held this same seat by about 6.4 against a strong, well-funded challenger (Clarke Tucker) in a roughly D+8.6 national environment, which is close to today's D+8 to D+9 (national_2026.csv aggregates, 2026-10-30).
- No outside-spending surge for Jones (DCCC or HMP independent expenditures) was found. Hill's cash on hand is 5.4x Jones's.
- One point for Jones: the Hendrix sample is 12% Black, which may understate the Black share of the electorate in a Little Rock seat. I could not verify the actual share.

**Note:** The "underrates underdog" claim rests on a single 2.5-month-old SMS poll from a pollster with a known D lean in this seat. The newest data point favours Hill, and every rater says Likely R. A probability of 0.17-0.20 for Jones is defensible, so I would not take the Jones side.
