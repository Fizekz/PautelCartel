# CA-34 (Los Angeles: downtown, Boyle Heights, Eagle Rock, Highland Park). Same-party top-two general, D vs D

**Kalshi Nov 1 (KXCAELECTION-2634):** Angela Gonzales-Torres (D, DSA/Justice Democrats) mid 0.805 (normalised 0.821), Jimmy Gomez (D, incumbent) 0.175. Volume about $80.9k.
**Verdict:** market_about_right. q(Gomez wins) = 0.22. Confidence: medium-low.

## Structure
- California top-two general, so both finalists are Democrats. There is no Republican on the ballot. The seat is D+28 (Harris won 76.5% of the two-party vote).
- 2026 primary (June 2): Gomez 46.0%, Gonzales-Torres 30.7%, Calvin Lee (R) 13.6%, Lucero (D) 5.1%, Dixon (D) 3.4%, Colin (I) 1.1%. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California%27s_34th_congressional_district (accessed 2026-11-01).
- In 2024 Gomez beat David Kim (D) only 55.6-44.4 in the D-vs-D general, so he had a weak record even before the scandal.
- The roughly 14% Republican primary vote has no candidate of its own. These voters may skip the race or lean toward the more moderate incumbent. That is a structural cushion for Gomez that the polls may not capture.
- The district is new-map California (Prop 50), but CA-34 is still an LA urban Democratic seat.

## Reasoning
Every poll has Gonzales-Torres ahead or tied, including the only independent one (USC, leaned 51-45). The incumbent faces an active ethics probe and an assault allegation, and he has stopped campaigning in public. That supports her being the clear favourite. Pulling the other way, the USC initial ballot was 34-34 with about 30% undecided, and its email mode is disputed. Gomez also has about 14% orphaned Republican primary voters who may lean his way, and much more cash. Overall I put Gonzales-Torres at about 78%, which is close to the market's 80-82%. I have no specific evidence that the market is wrong.

## Skeptic review
*Independent skeptic pass, 2026-11-01. Web search budget was exhausted early, so most checks used direct fetches.*

**Verdict: UPHELD.** My q_underdog (Gomez) is 0.23. Kalshi has about 0.175 raw (0.179 normalised), and the dossier has 0.22.

**Verified:**
- USC poll: initial result 34-34, 51-45 for Gonzales-Torres with leaners. The Gomez campaign disputes the email mode. Source: qz (updated 2026-10-29). The thedemocracylab.com page did not render for me, so the dates and n=423 rest on polls_2026.csv (VoteHub).
- Upswing/Guardrails poll: 41-38, MoE 4.9, dates and n not stated (Semafor, 2026-09-11).
- Jewish Insider (2026-10-26): internal polls had her ahead by 3 in early September and by 10 in early October. UDP (AIPAC) has not appeared.
- Wikipedia CA-34: 2024 result 55.6-44.4; primary result 46.0 / 30.7 / 13.6 (R).
- FEC numbers match fec_2026.csv.

**Factual errors:**
- "That primary came before the scandal went public" is imprecise. The Jimmy Gomez Wikipedia page (fetched 2026-11-01) and The Eastsider (2026-06-02) both say CNN and the LA Times reported the House Ethics probe on primary day, June 2. Gomez admitted "personal mistakes outside my marriage" that same day. Most mail ballots had already been cast, so the practical point mostly stands.
- The escalation the dossier dates to Aug 17 is the sexual-assault allegation. CNN reported a former staffer's assault claim on Aug 19. An April 2026 NY Post kissing allegation also predates the primary.

**Missed evidence:**
- American Prospect (2026-09-28, https://prospect.org/?p=145395):
  - The scandal has kept outside groups out of the race. Leading the Future endorsed Gomez; AIPAC-linked groups spent nearly $3M for him in 2024, and Fairshake about $500k.
  - Gomez is sending franked official mail.
  - State-party delegates petitioned to rescind his party endorsement.
- Semafor (2026-09-11): in the same Upswing poll, Gonzales-Torres led 63-22 after voters were told Leading the Future backs Gomez. This shows the AI-money attack has bite.

**Note:** The evidence for Gonzales-Torres is consistent: every poll has her ahead or tied, the incumbent is absent from the campaign, and the money that saved him in 2024 has not come. Gomez's upside comes from the 34-34 initial ballot, the 14% orphaned R primary voters, and possible bias from an email-mode sample in a heavily Latino and Asian, lower-propensity electorate. Both the market and the dossier are about right. A small lean toward Gomez upside is not enough to act on.
