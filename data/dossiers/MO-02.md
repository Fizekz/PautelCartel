## Structure
- The 2025 GOP map was suspended by referendum petition, so the general election uses the 2022/2023 lines (local redistricting file). Proposition A, the referendum on that map, is on the Nov 3 ballot and could boost turnout.
- 2024 two-party presidential vote: Trump 54.0 / Harris 46.0 (PVI R+4). Wagner won 54.5% in 2024.
- A Libertarian, Brandon Daugherty, is also on the ballot. Plurality rules.

## Reasoning
Uniform swing from the 2024 result (R+8) under a D+8 to D+9 environment gives roughly a tied race. All three fall polls have the race within 1 point or show Wellman ahead, and the one non-partisan poll has Wellman +5. Two rating changes this month moved toward Wellman. Against that, Wagner has a 7:1 cash-on-hand edge. She also has a track record of beating close polls: in 2020, polls showed a close race and she beat Jill Schupp by about 6 points. Inside Elections and Cook still lean clearly R. I think the race is close to a coin flip. A 63% price for Wagner looks about 8 points too high, but the evidence (n=401 Emerson, a D-sponsored PPP poll) is thin.

## Skeptic review
Date: 2026-11-01. Verdict: **weakened**. The direction holds up, but the size of the edge rests on thin evidence. Skeptic q_underdog (Wellman) = 0.42, against Kalshi 0.365 and the dossier's 0.45.

**Checks**
- All four polls match the local polls file, which mirrors Wikipedia. The PPP poll (HMP-sponsored, Oct 19-20, n=631) traces to Mark Maxwell on X (Oct 20). The tweet adds: "10% of Trump voters go Wellman. 6% of Harris voters go Wagner"; "Very excited" voters favour Wellman 53-43; and it gives Trump +7 in 2024.
- The Emerson/Poll Vault post "Levee Math" is dated Oct 21 in the Wikipedia citation, not Oct 22. This is minor.
- Ratings: the DCCC release of Oct 27 confirms Sabato's move to Toss-up. Cook is still Likely R (STLPR, Oct 30).

**Errors and caveats**
- The claim that independent expenditures are negligible (about $2.5k) is probably an artifact of lag in the FEC aggregates.
  - The DCCC release (Oct 27, partisan) is headlined "Republicans Spend Millions to Save Wagner", but it gives no amount. https://dccc.org/%F0%9F%9A%A8-ratings-change-sabatos-crystal-ball-moves-mo-02-to-toss-up-as-republicans-spend-millions-to-save-wagner/
  - KCUR (Oct 28) and STLPR (Oct 30) describe an aggressive, mostly negative Wagner ad campaign.
  - The FEC API was rate-limited when I tried it, so I could not verify any figures. Do not treat outside money as absent.
- The 7:1 cash-on-hand ratio is as of Oct 14. Wagner had already outspent her receipts ($5.67M spent vs $4.19M raised).

**Missed evidence**
- STLPR, 2026-10-30: "Silver Bulletin ranks it as the most competitive House race in the country." Kondik (Crystal Ball): "I think this is going to be a pretty close race." https://www.stlpr.org/government-politics-issues/2026-10-30/missouri-congress-ann-wagner-fred-wellman-race-toss-up
- KMOV/First Alert 4, 2026-10-21: more than 23,000 early ballots were cast in St. Louis County in two days. Wagner "has never won by less than three points" here. https://www.firstalert4.com/2026/10/22/seven-term-congresswoman-ann-wagner-locked-tight-race-with-democrat-fred-wellman/
- Newsmax, 2026-10-30: MO GOP chair Peter Kinder said "We think she's OK now." I only saw this in a search snippet because the page timed out, so treat it as unverified. https://www.newsmax.com/john-gizzi/missouri-ann-wagner-fred-wellman/2026/10/30/id/1274633/
- A search snippet put Polymarket at about 63% Wagner. I could not verify the date, but it is consistent with Kalshi.

**Assessment**
The best fall evidence is one non-partisan poll with n=401, plus two partisan polls that show a tie. A Republican internal showing a tie is somewhat telling. Silver Bulletin calling it the most competitive House race is the strongest independent support for the underdog case.

On the other side are Wagner's record of beating close polls (2020), her ad advantage, and two markets that agree near 63%. I lean slightly toward Wellman being underpriced (about 0.42), but not by the dossier's 8 points.
