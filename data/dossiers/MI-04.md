## Reasoning
The market has the challenger at 61.5% in a race every major rater calls a Tossup, consistent with its general wave pricing (NY-17 Tossup is 62% D; MI-07 Tossup is 78% D). The newest public poll, sponsored by a progressive group, has the Republican incumbent up 3. The previous poll, McCann's own, had him up only 1. Outside money is roughly balanced. A uniform swing from the MI statewide Senate polls puts the district near even before any incumbency edge. McCann may still win, since he has the cash advantage and the D+8 environment. But the race-level evidence does not support him being a 3:2 favourite. I put Huizenga at about 0.45. The main evidence is one poll with a wide margin of error, so confidence is low.

**Verdict: WEAKENED.** Skeptic q(Huizenga) = 0.42 (dossier 0.45; market 0.385).

**Checks**

  - RedBox (A Fight Worth Having), Oct 20–23, 787 LV, MOE ±6.6: Huizenga 47, McCann 44, other 5 (Barnett (G) 2), undecided 4.

  - Ratings: all Tossup (Cook Oct 9, IE Oct 29, DDHQ Oct 30, Sabato Sep 16, Silver Bulletin Aug 15); Economist Lean R (May 22).
  - FEC figures as stated.
- The late-October MI-SEN average of about El-Sayed +5 is confirmed in repo polls_2026.csv. The 11 polls since Oct 12 run from +1 to +7.

**Factual errors and nuances**
- Outside money is not "roughly balanced". Per legis1 (2026-10-23), the R side has about $6.46M: No Going Back $4.74M against McCann plus $0.35M for Huizenga, and Michigan Conservative Action $1.37M. The D side has about $4.69M: HMP $2.87M and LCV $1.82M. That is an R edge of about $1.8M, which slightly helps the dossier. https://legis1.com/news/michigan-4th-district-conservative-action-spends
- The RedBox ±6.6% MOE at n=787 implies a heavy design effect. The poll's effective sample is small, so it is weaker evidence than its n suggests.

**Missed evidence (refutes the "race-specific" reading of the RedBox poll)**
- RedBox released a whole batch of district polls with the same field dates, Oct 20–23 (repo polls_2026.csv, from Wikipedia). They consistently run more R than the market and the raters:
  - MI-10: Bouchard (R) 47, Hines 42. Market Hines 0.765.
  - NC-01: Davis +1. Market 0.845 D.
  - MT-01: Flint (R) +2. Market Forstag 0.585.
  - PA-01: tied. Market 0.535 D.
  - PA-10: Stelson +3. Market 0.815 D.
- The best same-state comparator is MI-10: same pollster, same dates, also Tossup-rated. RedBox shows R+5 there and the market still has the Democrat at 76.5%.
- So the MI-04 R+3 reading fits a batch-wide house effect, not a McCann-specific weakness. Its statewide MI-SEN +5 matching the consensus does not clear its district polls.
- Michigan Public (2026-10-14) reports that Cook moved MI-04 from Lean R to Tossup, a move toward D in October. https://www.michiganpublic.org/politics-government/2026-10-14/pete-buttigieg-stumps-for-sean-mccann-at-a-packed-campaign-event-in-kalamazoo
- A uniform national swing points both ways. Applied to Trump's 2024 two-party margin of R+5.6 (lean_2026.csv), a roughly 10-point swing gives about D+4. Applied to Huizenga's 2024 House margin of about 12 (Michigan Public), it gives about R+2. Incumbency is the real argument for Huizenga, and the dossier understates it.

**Net:** The case for Huizenga rests on one poll from a batch with a visible R tilt against consensus. The remaining arguments (Tossup ratings, R money edge, incumbency) are a generic "market too bullish on D Tossups" claim, not evidence specific to this race. I still put Huizenga a little above the market (0.42), but the "market_underrates_underdog" call is thinly supported.
