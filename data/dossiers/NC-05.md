## Reasoning
The national environment is about D+9 on the generic ballot. Applied as a uniform PVI swing, that would make an R+9 seat competitive, which is why the market prices Hubbard at about 18%. Cook's late move to Likely R confirms the seat is now on the board. The district-specific evidence is weak, though. Hubbard lost by 19 points in 2024, he has one-twentieth of Foxx's cash, there are no polls, no outside money and no DCCC investment. A low-elasticity rural Appalachian electorate usually swings less than suburbs. Six of ten raters still call it Safe or Solid R. On balance I estimate about 15%, slightly below the thin market's 18.5%. The gap is within noise, so I do not call it a mispricing.
