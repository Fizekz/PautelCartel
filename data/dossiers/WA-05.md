## Reasoning
The concrete evidence is the primary. An incumbent drew 47.4%, and 52.6% voted for someone else in a primary that historically runs close to the general.

If Powell's labor-backed vote breaks mostly to Conroy, this is a mid-single-digit race. The core raters moved from Solid to Likely R in October.

Against that, Conroy has little money and no visible outside support. The Economist and Split Ticket models still say Safe R, and some Powell voters may be anti-establishment Republicans.

I put Conroy at about 20% against the market's 15.5-16%. That is a modest gap, and confidence is low because there has been no fall polling.

## Skeptic review
_Independent check, 2026-11-01. Web search was used up, so this review relies on WebFetch of Spokesman-Review pages, current Wikipedia wikitext (WA page rev 1383008130), and the repo data files._

**Verdict: refuted.** The case that the market underrates Conroy does not hold up. My q_underdog for Conroy is **0.13**, against the market's 0.155.

**Claims verified**
- **The Aug 4 primary figures are correct** (Wikipedia election box):
  - Baumgartner 47.4%, the only R on the ballot.
  - Democrats 31.9% combined; Conroy 18.8%.
  - Powell (IPW) 16.4%.
  - Independents (Danimus, Hayes, Usrey, Bartleson) plus write-ins about 4.2%.
  - 204,209 votes in total.
- **Ratings, Sep 29 vs Nov 1, confirmed** from the wikitext. FiftyPlusOne moved Lean R to Likely R, toward the Republican.
- **The Tavern poll exists** as stated (May 18-25). It was commissioned by a PAC tied to Powell, which had an incentive to make Conroy look weak.

**Errors and nuances**
- **Primary benchmark for 2024.** In the 2024 top-two primary, Republicans combined 61.0% and Democrats 39.0%. The general was 60.6-39.3, so the primary predicted the general almost exactly (2024 WA page, election boxes).
- **What the 2026 primary implies.** It splits R 47.4 / D 31.9 / independents and others 20.6.
  - If the independent vote splits 60/40 toward the Democrat, Baumgartner wins by about 11.
  - Conroy needs roughly 75-80% of Powell's and the other independents' votes to win.
  - Powell's coalition (Forward Party, Yang, Osborn, Novogratz, building-trades unions) has a sizeable centre-right component. That kind of lopsided break is unlikely.

**Missed evidence (decisive)**
- **No Democratic money.** Spokesman-Review, Oct 22 2026: "Baumgartner, Conroy burn through campaign cash as election looms" (https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/oct/22/baumgartner-conroy-burn-through-campaign-cash-as-e/).
  - National and state Democratic group money is "noticeably absent."
  - Conroy spent about $377k since June, versus more than $940k for Baumgartner.
  - Baumgartner made eight ad buys of $50k or more, the largest $127k on Oct 9.
  - At the end of September Conroy had about $154k cash against Baumgartner's $800k.
- **Closing coverage.** Spokesman-Review, Oct 31 2026 (https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/oct/31/baumgartner-conroy-make-last-sometimes-dubious-pit/) says Baumgartner "is still the favorite." Several analysts moved the seat Safe to Likely, and the state party is paying for pro-Baumgartner mailers. It gives no polls and no Powell endorsement.
- **Debate coverage.** The Spokesman-Review, Sep 12 2026, covered the Sep 9 debate. It contains nothing on Powell endorsing anyone.
- **Peer check.** Kalshi already prices WA-05 (0.155) in line with AR-02 (0.175) and KS-02 (0.195) at similar ratings.

**Net:** The Solid-to-Likely rating moves are already in the price. The evidence that would be needed for an upset (Democratic outside spending, fall polling, a Powell endorsement) is absent, and local reporting says the Democratic money is not there. If anything the favourite is slightly underpriced.
