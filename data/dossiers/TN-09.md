## Structure
- New 2026 map. Two-party 2024 presidential vote of Trump 60.7 / Harris 39.3 (Downballot); Cook PVI R+9. The district holds part of Memphis plus GOP-leaning territory.
- Two independents, Dennis Clark and Michelle Head. Plurality election.

## Judgment
Even a large D swing of about 11 points from 2024 leaves this seat around R+10. Pearson is a well-funded progressive with strong Black-turnout potential in his part of Memphis, but even his allies' own poll had him down 4. Partisan internals usually overstate their side.

National Republicans are barely spending, and the experts are nearly unanimous at Likely/Solid R. Pearson needs a polling miss of roughly 8-10 points. At 15.5% the market is slightly generous to him; I put him at about 12%.

**Unverified or errors**
- The primary percentages (Pearson 65.7-24.3, Taylor 46%) and the HB 7003 details could not be verified from the sources I could reach.
- On the Taylor allegations: Call the Map's page (Oct 31) says the Nashville Scene reported accusations that Taylor sent unsolicited nude pictures. It also says Taylor acknowledged an "indiscretion" (per Tennessee Lookout), called it "youthful indiscretions", and that state Sen. Charlane Oliver called him "crass" and a "bully". I could not fetch the original stories, so treat this as second-hand. The allegations appear to be old conduct and have not moved any rater toward Pearson except Inside.
- The fundraising figures mix two tables. Wikipedia's Oct 15 table shows Taylor raised $2.69M with $1.54M in loans, and Pearson $3.64M. The FEC figures through Oct 14 in repo fec_2026.csv are $2.83M and $4.01M. Same direction either way.

**Missed evidence**
- PoliAgg's model gives *Pearson* 99% (D+27.5) (https://poliagg.com/race/us-house-2026-tn-9/, Oct 31). It is plainly using the old Memphis-based lines. Ignore it, and don't treat PoliAgg elsewhere as an independent check.
- 2018 base rate from repo results and lean data: seats with this swing-adjusted lean (about -8 points two-party) went Democratic only about 2-16% of the time in a D+8.6 year.
- Endorsements for Pearson include Kamala Harris, Chris Murphy, the NEA and the Tennessee AFL-CIO (Wikipedia, Oct 31). They signal turnout effort, not polling movement.

**Bottom line**: a Trump +21 seat, with the only poll from Pearson allies showing him down 4, minimal Republican outside spending (they evidently see no threat), and near-unanimous Likely/Solid R ratings. Turnout upside in Memphis and the Taylor stories keep this from being negligible. I put Pearson at 11% against the market's 15.5%, a gap too small to call the market wrong. Under the contest scoring the break-even q for picking Pearson is about 0.082.
