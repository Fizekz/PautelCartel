- The Press Democrat endorsed Thompson (Oct 6).
- Ro Khanna endorsed Jones in late October (Press Democrat / Axios via Davis Vanguard).
- Jones ads say Thompson "plans to die in office." Former Thompson aides protested and Thompson denies saying it (SF Chronicle).
- Generational framing: Jones is 35; Thompson has served since 1999 (Press Democrat, Oct 16).

## Structure
California top-two, Democrat vs Democrat. Harris won 57.5% two-party on the new lines (PVI D+8). Prop 50 added conservative parts of Colusa, Placer, Sacramento, Sutter and Yuba counties. Republicans, about 42% of 2024 presidential voters, have no candidate of their own, so they are the swing bloc. About 21K Republican primary votes in Yuba and Sutter are in play.

## Reasoning
Same-party California races where the challenger courts the other party's voters and outspends the incumbent have unseated incumbents before: Swalwell over Stark in 2012, Khanna over Honda in 2016. Here Jones:
- outspends Thompson about 3:1,
- has the endorsement of the third-place Republican and is courting Republicans openly,
- benefits from Thompson's weak 41% primary share.

Jones's two October internals show him up 10-13, after the same firm reportedly had him down 13 in July. Sponsor bias and selective release could account for 5+ points, which would still leave Jones ahead. Thompson has released no counter-poll. Against Jones: Thompson's incumbency, local Republican leaders and the Democratic base in Napa and Sonoma. The "die in office" ads could also backfire. The market's 53.5% for Thompson seems to lean on incumbency. I put Jones at about 56%. The evidence is internal polls only, so confidence is limited.

## Skeptic review
*Independent skeptic, 2026-11-01. Web search budget ran out partway through; checks used WebFetch.*

**Verdict: weakened.** The claim that the market underrates Jones rests only on Jones-paid polls.

**Checks**
- Napa Valley Register (2026-10-29): Binder Oct 15-21, n=600, Jones 52-40; Fulcrum Oct 18-20, n=400, Jones 53-40. The July Binder poll showing Thompson +13 is known only from a Jones release.
- SFGATE/Yahoo (2026-10-28) reports the same Binder poll as **Jones 45, Thompson 35**, with fieldwork described only as "in October" and a margin of error of 2.8-4%.
- The NVR article does not say whether 52-40 is a pushed-leaner ballot or an informed (post-message) ballot. If it is an informed ballot, it is close to worthless as a forecast.
- SFGATE says the NYT cited no polls in the district.
- Press Democrat (2026-10-31) confirms: both polls were Jones-funded; Jones has put in $15M of his own money; New Leadership Now PAC is running attack ads on Thompson; neither the Yuba nor the Sutter GOP has endorsed; Riehle endorsed Jones; Thompson's support in Sonoma and Napa "appears secure". It calls the race one "that could be surprisingly close", not one Jones leads.

**Errors and overstatements**
- The dossier treats 52-40 as the main number. The initial ballot (45-35) is the more defensible one.
- "Even a 5+ point discount still leaves Jones ahead" applies a discount to the strongest available framing of a campaign's own release.
- A campaign that announces it was down 13 in July is releasing polls selectively.

**Missed evidence**
- CalMatters (2026-09-29): Jones ran his primary on young progressive voters, then pivoted to Republicans and centrists. His platform includes a corporate tax hike and $10K-a-month refundable credits, a mismatch for Sutter and Yuba Republicans.
- CalMatters (2026-09-29): Thompson is a traditional Blue Dog with 27 years of incumbency and Ways and Means seniority. That profile is natural for Republicans with no candidate of their own.
- Press Democrat (2026-10-31): about 21K June Republican ballots in Yuba and Sutter are "up for grabs", and some Republican voters may skip the race.

**Skeptic arithmetic**
- Assume Democrats split about 65/35 for Thompson and Thompson's base holds.
- On those assumptions, Jones needs roughly 75-80% of the Republicans who vote in the race. That is possible given the Riehle endorsement and the money, but it is a high bar.
- P(Jones) is about 0.48. The market's 0.465 is close, and the evidence for moving it is thin.
