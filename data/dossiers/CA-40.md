## Structure
California top-two, Republican vs Republican. PVI R+6; Trump 56.3% two-party (2024, new lines). Democrats and NPP voters, a larger share in November than in the June primary, will decide it, but many will undervote.

## Reasoning
Calvert won the primary by 14 points on the strength of Republican voters in Riverside County. In November, the larger Democratic and independent electorate should lean toward Kim: she has the more moderate brand, Calvert has attacked her from the right, and she has the money edge. A rough model has Calvert winning Republicans about 60/40 and the roughly 70% of Democrats who vote breaking about 70/30 for Kim. That gives about a 50/50 race, slightly favouring Kim if Democratic turnout is high (D+8.5 environment). The only polls are sponsor-linked, and the latest (September) has Kim +9. The market's 52/48 for Kim looks right. I have no strong evidence to move it.

## Skeptic review
*Independent skeptic, 2026-11-01. Web search budget ran out partway through; checks used WebFetch.*

**Verdict: upheld (market about right).** I lean slightly more toward Calvert than the dossier does.

**Checks**
- Ballotpedia (2026-06-12) confirms the primary: Calvert 35%, Kim 21%.
- NBC LA (2026-10-09, updated 10-28) confirms that Riverside County backed Calvert "overwhelmingly" and the two drew similar support in Orange County. It also says both Republicans have distanced themselves from Trump.
- Spectrum (2026-10-28) confirms the 96K Democrat and NPP primary voters and the quotes from Singleton (voted Kim) and Kephart (won't back either).
- Kim's July 24 release confirms the July poll, Calvert 33, Kim 32. It says a pro-Kim super PAC circulated the memo.

**Errors and overstatements**
- The dossier says the Sep 5-9 Grassroots Targeting poll's sponsor is "not confirmed". The repo's polls_2026.csv lists the same sponsor, "California First Fund", for both the July and September polls. That points to the Kim-aligned super PAC, so the Kim +9 poll should be treated as a pro-Kim internal.
- The Politico source could not be fetched, and I could not verify it independently.

**Missed evidence**
- Calvert currently represents 51% of the new district and Kim 35% (ABC7, via Ballotpedia, 2026-06-12). Calvert has the larger incumbency base.
- The new CA-40 is 10 points more Republican than Kim's old seat (same source).

**Skeptic view**
- The primary split among Republicans was about 62/38 for Calvert.
- If that holds, Kim needs roughly 70-75% of the Democrats and NPP voters who mark the race. Undervoting makes that bar higher.
- That is plausible, given her moderate brand, the attacks on her from the right and her money edge, but not assured.
- P(Calvert) is about 0.49.
