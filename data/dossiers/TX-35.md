# TX-35: Johnny Garcia (D) vs Carlos De La Cruz (R), redrawn 2025 Texas map, open seat

**Kalshi Nov 1:** Garcia 0.735 (normalised 0.75), De La Cruz 0.245. Underdog: De La Cruz.
**Judgement:** market about right, with a lean toward the underdog. q_underdog (De La Cruz) = 0.33. Confidence: medium-low.

## Structure
- Mid-decade Texas map. The new TX-35 covers San Antonio's Southside, southern Bexar County, the northeast suburbs near Randolph AFB, and rural, Republican Wilson, Guadalupe and Karnes counties. About 60% of the vote is in Bexar.
- Trump won the new district by 10 points in 2024. Cruz would have won it by 1 point in 2018 and 4 points in 2024 (Texas Tribune, 10-06).
- The eligible voter population is 51.8% Hispanic, but Spanish-surname voters were only 37% of the 2024 electorate.
- The old TX-35 incumbent, Greg Casar, now runs in TX-37.
- Two-candidate race; Cook PVI R+4.

## Ratings (as of 10-27/30)

## Money
- FEC through 10-14: Garcia raised $2.20M and has $0.15M cash on hand. De La Cruz raised $2.02M and has $0.31M cash on hand.
- Outside spending as of the Texas Tribune on 10-06: Republicans had spent or booked $4.4M in general-election ads, $4M of it from the Trump-tied No Going Back PAC. Democrats had $5.1M, mostly from HMP.

## News
- Speaker Johnson called the seat "the whole enchilada".
- The Trump-affiliated PAC is attacking Garcia over a 2019 jail suicide-attempt case.
- Trump rallied in San Antonio with De La Cruz and Paxton (TT 10-06; TPR).
- 2026-10-09: TPR debate coverage. De La Cruz called Trump's tariffs "way too broad".
- Garcia is a Blue Dog Democrat and a Bexar sheriff's deputy. UTSA's Jon Taylor expects a 2-3 point race that Garcia wins if Bexar turnout is high.
- Polymarket had Garcia at 66% on 10-23 (PoliAgg/aggregator).
- I found no early-vote reports.

## Reasoning
All three October polls are within 2 points, including the nonpartisan WaPo/Schar poll showing De La Cruz +1. That is a closer race than Kalshi's 73.5-75% implies. The D+8-9 national environment and Garcia's moderate profile argue for a few points of D edge on fundamentals. Blending polls (about D+1) with fundamentals (about D+5) gives roughly D+3, which implies about 65-70% for Garcia. That is slightly below the market, so I see a modest lean toward De La Cruz but not enough specific evidence to call the market wrong. A Hispanic-turnout miss could cut either way, as it did in 2024 (R) and 2025 (D).
