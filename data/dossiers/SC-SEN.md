## Reasoning
The polling average is about Graham +1.4, with nonpartisan polls ranging from a tie (NYT) to Graham +4 (ECU). The R-sponsored polls show Graham +1 to +4, and the D-sponsored polls show Andrews +1 to +5. Andrews has a huge money edge and Graham is a weak first-time candidate, both of which favour Andrews. Against that, SC is R+8 and Senate polls in deep-red states have tended to underestimate Republicans; this is general background, not a sourced figure. A roughly 1.5-point polling lead plus some red-state polling bias implies about 68-72% for Graham. That matches the market, so I see no specific edge and lean only slightly toward Andrews (0.30).

**Missed evidence**
- **Early vote.** SC early voting set a midterm record. WRDW (2026-10-30) reports 838,420 ballots through Oct 29, and the State Election Commission director says that is about double 2022: https://www.wrdw.com/2026/10/30/over-835k-votes-cast-sc-sees-record-early-voting-turnout/
  - The final figure was close to 1M (WRDW update, 10-31).
  - Top counties: Greenville 83k, Charleston 79k, Richland 74k, Horry 72k.
  - SC has no party registration, so the partisan direction cannot be read. High turnout is ambiguous here.
- **Undecided voters.** Polls with many undecideds tend to show Graham further ahead (Citadel LV: 15% undecided, Graham +4). In an R+8 state, undecideds leaning R is the usual pattern.
- **Outside spending.** I found no outside-spending totals, and I could not search further.
- **Graham's fundraising looks oddly low.** Her FEC total of $1.92M is small for a sitting appointed senator. It may reflect a new committee. I could not verify this.

**Reasoning.** Nonpartisan polls run from a tie to Graham +4, and the averages put her at +0.1 to +2.7. SC polls historically overstated Democrats; in 2020, Lindsey Graham beat his polling average by several points. That is background from memory, not re-sourced. Against that, the national environment is D+9 and Graham is a weak candidate. About 70% for Graham is reasonable, and I see nothing specific that shows the market is wrong.
