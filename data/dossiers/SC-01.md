- Early voting ran Oct 19-31. Charleston County's first day beat the 2022 first day (9,389 votes). South Carolina has no party registration, so there is no partisan early-vote read.

## Structure
Plurality vote, 2024 map (the post-Callais redraw was not passed). Trump 56.6% two-party in 2024; PVI R+6. Minor-party candidates take a few points. The district is coastal and educated (Charleston and Beaufort suburbs) and has trended toward Democrats.

## Reasoning
A uniform swing from about R+17 in 2024 in a D+8.5 environment gives roughly R+6. Lacore's strong profile, her 4x hard-money edge and an educated electorate narrow that further. After adjusting the internals for sponsor bias (about 3-4 points), the partisan polls average roughly R+1 to R+2. Cook and Inside both moved toward Lacore in the final days. Against that, outside spending favours Honeycutt and she has Trump's endorsement. That points to a real coin flip, which is what the market shows (51/49). I have no specific evidence that the price is wrong.

**Missed evidence (leans slightly Lacore)**
- Mount Pleasant's Republican mayor, Will Haynie, and former Charleston mayor John Tecklenburg endorsed Lacore (Wikipedia SC House page, read 2026-11-01).
- The Post and Courier editorial board endorsed Lacore on 2026-10-22 (same source).
- A statewide NYT/Siena poll, Oct 19-26, LV n=707, shows Republicans 50, Democrats 41 (Wikipedia SC House page). That is roughly a 9-point swing toward Democrats from Trump's 2024 statewide margin of about 18. Applied to SC-01's R+13 presidential baseline, it leaves about R+4 before candidate effects.
- Candidate effects plausibly favour Lacore: a 4x hard-money edge and endorsements that cross party lines.
