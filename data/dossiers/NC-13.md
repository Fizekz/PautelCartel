- The national environment is about D+9.
- The early-vote surge in NC is huge.
- Kalshi and Polymarket agree at about 59-60% Knott, so this is not just thin-market noise.

For Knott:
- Uniform swing from Trump +16.6 gives about R+6, plus a sophomore incumbent against a challenger hit with carpetbagger attacks.
- Every rater is Likely or Safe R.
- There is no public or internal poll showing it close. Democrats released no poll, even though they did in other targets.
- In the 2018 analogue, R seats in this position lost about 20-25% of the time.
- Within NC, Kalshi prices NC-13 below comparable seats such as NC-14 (0.77) and NC-09 (0.67).

I estimate Knott at about 68%. The market looks somewhat too low on him, but two markets agree and the early-vote signal is real, so confidence is low.

## Skeptic review
_Independent skeptic, 2026-11-01. My web-search budget ran out partway through. The rest of this review uses direct page fetches only._

**Verdict: weakened** (the claim that the market underrates Knott is not supported strongly enough). Skeptic q_underdog (Barringer) = 0.36. The market price is 0.405.

**Claims checked**
- Pollscape (retrieved 2026-11-01) shows no public poll. The only NC-13 "polling memo" online is a May 2024 GOP primary runoff poll (Differentiators Data, Knott 74, Daughtry 17), which is irrelevant here.
- Pollscape shows Polymarket at R 58.5% / D 41.5% (record of 2026-11-01 08:56 GMT), confirming the two markets agree.
- Roll Call 10/31 confirms Inside moved Solid R to Likely R. Its reasoning was general: Trump's approval, Democratic strength in NC, and GOP-drawn maps "not performing as Republicans expected".
- The Assembly 2026-10-20, after five early-vote days, confirms D in-person ballots up 53% and R down about 5% versus 2022. It adds that **turnout among registered D was 14.1% versus 6.5% among R (2022: 8.6% vs 7.2%)**, and that Black turnout was up 70%.
- WRAL confirms about 2.6M early ballots through 10/30, with D plus unaffiliated about three-quarters of them.

**Errors / unsourced**
- The figure that "R seats like this lost about 20-25% of the time in 2018" has no source and cannot be checked. I give it no weight.
- WUNC 2026-10-27 is more than a headline. Knott is one of seven NC GOP incumbents (with Rouzer, Murphy, McDowell, Hudson, Moore and Foxx) now running TV ads in deep-red seats. His ad calls Barringer a Marylander. WUNC notes Barringer is a **Sanford native** who recently moved back from DC, which weakens the carpetbagger attack.
- The NC-14 (0.77) and NC-09 (0.67) comparison is selective. Kalshi prices every NC Republican seat with a state-specific D discount: NC-03 (R+6) R 0.555, NC-11 (R+5) D 0.845, NC-01 D 0.845. NC-13 at 0.595 fits that pattern.

**Missed evidence**
- In lean_2026.csv, NC-13's Democratic two-party presidential share *rose* from 41.25% (2020) to 41.68% (2024) while the nation moved right. NC-09 fell from 42.96% to 42.00%. NC-13 is a growing exurban seat around Johnston and Wake, the kind of electorate that swings toward Democrats in midterms. That justifies pricing it below NC-09 at the same PVI.
- Raters and commentary point to NC in particular as a place where Democrats are overperforming (Roll Call 10/31). The early-vote party data (D turnout rate twice the R rate) is the strongest district-relevant hard data available, and it points toward Barringer.

**Assessment.** Fundamentals alone (R+16.6 in 2024 presidential margin, a sophomore incumbent, no polling) would justify Knott at about 75% or more, and R+8 seats outside NC trade at 0.82-0.95. Two independent markets, Inside's NC-specific reasoning, and the NC early-vote turnout gap all explain the discount, though. I put Knott at about 64%, only slightly above the market, with low confidence. That is not enough to back the favourite with conviction.
