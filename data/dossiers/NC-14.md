# NC-14: Tim Moore (R, inc.) vs LaKesha Womack (D)

**Kalshi (2026-11-01 snapshot):** Moore 0.765 mid (0.773 normalized), Womack 0.225.
**Judgment:** the market underrates the favourite. q(Womack) = 0.12. Confidence: medium.

## Polls
- I found no poll since 2026-09-15. The only public poll is Ragnar Research (R, for Conservatives for America), Mar 10-12 2026, 400 LV: Moore 48, Womack 40. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_North_Carolina

## Ratings
- Ratings as of Oct 27-30: Cook Solid R, Sabato Safe R, Inside Elections Solid R. Decision Desk HQ and the Economist have it at Likely R. None of the three main raters moved it in the last month (repo ratings_2026.csv, as_of 2026-10-30).

## Money
- FEC filings through Oct 14: Moore raised $2.09M and has $1.09M cash on hand. Womack raised $59K and has $4.8K on hand. I found no DCCC, HMP or other Democratic outside spending in this district.

## News
- On 2026-10-11, Womack was served with a criminal summons at a campaign rally in Polk County. The charge is a 2020 Class H felony for failing to return a rented car, and her court date is Nov 10. She has not been convicted. She says she was homeless at the time and did not know about the case. Moore's campaign is running ads calling it "stolen property." Sources: Spokesman-Review/AP, 2026-10-16, https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/oct/16/nc-democratic-congressional-nominee-served-with-fe/ and WFAE, 2026-10-29, https://www.wfae.org/politics/2026-10-29/meet-lakesha-womack-the-first-time-candidate-challenging-tim-moore
- WBTV reported that Womack bought a home outside the district. North Carolina does not require House candidates to live in the district (WFAE, 2026-10-29).
- Moore's own vulnerabilities, per WFAE: a personal affair lawsuit, a late stock-trade disclosure, and no town halls.
- Context: North Carolina's statewide environment looks strongly Democratic. Kalshi has NC-SEN at 97% D and NC-11 at 84.5% D. Daily Kos reports the GOP Senate effort has "all but given up," which could depress Republican turnout (2026-10-26).

## Structure
- Plurality voting. The 2025 S249 remap changed only NC-01 and NC-03, so this district's lines are unchanged (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina%27s_14th_congressional_district). Trump's 2024 2-party share was 57.6% (about R+15), and the PVI is R+8. Moore won 58.1-41.9 in 2024.

## Reasoning
Kalshi's 22.7% for Womack looks like a wave-driven price applied across North Carolina seats; NC-07 is priced almost the same. A D+9 environment could bring an R+8 seat into range on paper. But Womack has almost no money ($4.8K cash), no outside help that I could find, and a felony charge that surfaced three weeks before the election and is now the subject of attack ads. Every major rater still has the race at Solid or Safe R. Seats like this flip in waves only when the challenger is funded or the incumbent collapses, and neither is happening here. I put her chances at 10-13%.

**Missed evidence for the underdog (against the dossier)**
- North Carolina's statewide environment is extreme. Cooper leads Whatley by about 8 to 14 points in every October poll in data/clean/polls_2026.csv, including R-sponsored Harper (Oct 18-20, 49-39) and Quantus (Oct 24-25, 51-43). Trump won NC by about 3 in 2024. A uniform swing of the Senate margin onto NC-14 (Trump about +15) would bring the seat close to even. House races swing less than a Cooper-led top of ticket, but this is the strongest case for Womack.
- The district has trended toward Democrats: D 2-party share was 41.8% in 2020 and 42.4% in 2024 (data/clean/lean_2026.csv). Its Mecklenburg/Charlotte portion is the trend driver.
- The only district poll is the March 10-12 Ragnar (R) poll, and it had Moore at only 48-40 against an unknown challenger. That is weak for an R-sponsored poll.
- The NRCC is defending NC-09 (Hudson, Trump +16, R+8), which is slightly redder than NC-14 (Daily Kos, 2026-10-26).
