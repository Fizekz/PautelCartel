## Structure
2024 lines are still in use; SCOTUS blocked the NY-11 redraw. New York has fusion ballot lines. Hochul vs Blakeman for governor is at the top of the ticket.

## Judgment
Head-to-head polling is a dead heat. The model-based forecasters lean D, and the national environment is about D+8-9 on the generic ballot. In wave years, toss-ups tend to break toward the out-party: in 2018, Cook toss-ups held by Republican incumbents went R in only 8 of 29. Lawler's personal overperformance cuts the other way. Conley at about 62-63% fits all of this. There is no specific evidence that the price is wrong.

- McLaughlin for Lawler, Sep 28-30, n=400, +/-4.9: Lawler 50, Conley 47 (https://www.lawlerforcongress.com/2026/10/02/lawler-leads-cait-conley-50-47-in-new-poll-of-ny-17/, Oct 2).

- The 2018 base rate holds: Republican incumbents in Cook Toss-up seats won 8 of 29 (ratings_hist.csv x results_house.csv).

**Errors**
- The CBS New York article cited for the "Oct 25 Jeffries rally" is dated 2026-10-29. It says nothing about a Jeffries rally; it reports only the more than 126k early votes in Westchester, Rockland, Putnam and Dutchess, and that early voting ends Sunday. The rally claim is therefore unsourced.
- The City & State piece of Oct 5 gives no dollar figures for the Musk PAC or the DCCC, so the "heavy outside money" claim has no amounts behind it.

**Missed evidence**
- In the same Lawler internal (Sep 28-30), Blakeman leads Hochul 51-47 in NY-17. That suggests the top of the ticket may not lift Conley much there; it is a partisan source (Lawler campaign page, Oct 2).
- Emerson (Sep 27-29) has Hochul's job approval in the district at 40/49. Lawler's favourability is 45/46, down from 52/42 in October 2024. The first number cuts toward Lawler, the second toward Conley.
- City & State (Oct 5): Biden won these lines by 10 in 2020, and Democrats hold a registration edge of about 78k. CBS (Oct 29) quotes Cook's Erin Covey: Lawler's campaigning skill is "why the race is still close" despite an unpopular president.
- Fundamentals: Harris won 50.3% of the two-party vote (lean_2026.csv). A swing of about 5 two-party points to a D+8.5 environment, less Lawler's 2024 personal overperformance of about 3.5 points (he took 53.2% when Harris took 50.3%), leaves Conley at about 52%. The polls (about a tie) are slightly worse for Conley than that. The market at 0.635 sits between the two, so I see no mispricing.
